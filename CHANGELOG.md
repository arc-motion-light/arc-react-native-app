# ARC React Native App — Development Changelog

The engineering milestones behind ARC's connected application, developed alongside ARC firmware, hardware, and cloud services. This is a **development changelog** rather than a production-release history. Entries describe implementation and demonstrated results across the ARC ecosystem; source code for the wireless firmware and backend is maintained separately.

## Unreleased — October 2026

### Highlights

- **Hardware-to-cloud identity overhaul.** Established a consistent STM32 hardware-identity model across wireless firmware, native Bluetooth communication, the mobile app, and ARC account/device services. Physical MCU identity, product registration, and access permissions now have distinct roles.
- **Wireless platform modernization.** Migrated the ARC Probe T prototype to **ST STM32_WPAN 2.10.0**, with successful programming, startup, retained-history checks, and renewed sensor acquisition.
- **Mobile framework upgrade.** Advanced the shared application to **Expo SDK 57**, **React Native 0.86.3**, and **React 19.2.3**, retaining ARC's native hardware interfaces.
- **iOS and Android hardware milestones.** Extended the app's native capabilities on iPhone and demonstrated Android operation on a Samsung Galaxy Tab A8, including device communication and telemetry views.
- **Physical QR identification.** Scanned a Probe T label generated by the ARC Admin Panel on an iPhone, resolved its public hardware identity, and displayed a matching registered device from the ARC backend.
- **Probe data end to end.** Received a physical Probe T time-sync acknowledgement and live environmental measurements on iOS, stored a sample locally, and displayed the first recorded Probe graph point.
- **ARC Secure Core.** Validated the secure-session design in **35 host-side protocol checks**, alongside embedded footprint measurements and runtime memory profiling for the STM32WBA5 integration path.

### Hardware-rooted identity — coordinated firmware, app, and cloud upgrade

**Implemented and refined**

- Adopted the STM32WBA5's factory-programmed **96-bit hardware UID** as the canonical physical MCU identifier, serialized consistently across firmware messages, mobile application logic, and account/device records.
- Added a versioned BLE hardware-identity request/response contract, with validation of protocol version, device type, response length, request/session matching, and UID format.
- Reworked iOS identity resolution to distinguish a permanent **hardware UID** from a Bluetooth address, a CoreBluetooth peripheral identifier, or an OpenThread network address. Reconnection and local device selection no longer need to assume a stable transport address.
- Kept the existing **ARC Device ID** as the logical product identity used for registration, claims, account inventory, and user-facing associations. A hardware UID is linked to an ARC product through explicit, conflict-aware binding rules rather than display-name matching.
- Connected the mobile onboarding and account-facing workflows with ARC backend device records, hardware-binding resolution, and permission-aware access. Device ownership and sharing remain separate from physical identification.
- Extended the telemetry/history model toward permanent source provenance: **originating Probe UID and sequence** are distinguished from the **collecting Lamp UID and journal sequence**. Generation-aware identifiers are being introduced to prevent records from different device or journal incarnations being confused.
- Made hardware identity a shared foundation for device selection, QR review, history attribution, and the next ARC Secure Core integration phase.

**Verified**

- Independently read the STM32WBA5 factory UID using ST development tools and validated the intended canonical byte representation.
- Demonstrated hardware-identity-aware device resolution in the iOS development flow.
- On **13 October**, scanned an ARC Probe T label on an iPhone and confirmed that its decoded identity matched a device registered to the signed-in account through the ARC backend.
- The device review interface correctly separated **identity matching** from **ownership authorization** and future **encrypted local bonding**.

This upgrade spans multiple ARC components. It should not be confused with completed cryptographic key provisioning: **a factory UID identifies hardware, but is not itself a secret or an authentication credential**.

### Device onboarding and QR identification

**Added**

- Hardware-aware discovery, claimed-device selection, association review, and conflict-handling foundations for newly encountered ARC nodes.
- A **Scan QR** action in the iOS Devices interface, using `expo-camera` and just-in-time camera permission.
- Strict parsing for ARC's public identity-label format and a device review screen connected to registered inventory.
- React Native-compatible Base64URL decoding with canonical-format validation, following a mismatch discovered during the first physical iPhone scan.

**Verified — 13 October**

- The iPhone camera recognized the Admin Panel-generated QR label, decoded it successfully after the compatibility correction, and opened the registered Probe T identity review.
- The QR label functions as a public identification aid; possession of the label alone does not grant device control.

### STM32 wireless and firmware integration

**Verified — 12 October**

- Migrated the **ARC Probe T / STM32WBA5** prototype to **STM32_WPAN 2.10.0**, built and programmed the firmware, and confirmed successful startup.
- Preserved protected persistent regions, including relevant existing settings and retained history, across the update.
- Observed fresh sensor acquisition and another persistent historian entry after startup.
- Continued direct BLE and time-sync integration with the mobile application, culminating in a recorded Probe measurement visible in the iOS graph.

The Lamp's corresponding wireless-stack migration is a separate firmware milestone. Wireless firmware and its certification requirements are not part of this mobile app repository.

### Application platform and native development

**Upgraded**

- Moved to **Expo SDK 57 / React Native 0.86.3 / React 19.2.3**, updating the dependency set while preserving ARC-specific native BLE functionality.
- Integrated the Expo Camera and barcode-scanning native modules into the existing **Xcode workspace**, with a camera usage description in both native iOS and Expo configuration.
- Continued development of the shared TypeScript application with platform-specific integrations where hardware access requires them.

**Demonstrated**

- iOS distribution through **TestFlight** for invited testers, alongside ongoing direct hardware testing through Xcode.
- Android execution on a **Samsung Galaxy Tab A8 (Android 14)**, including native BLE communication with ARC hardware, lighting controls, live telemetry, cloud history, and graph presentation.
- A documented earlier checkpoint of **111 passing automated tests**, plus subsequent focused identity, history, and QR checks. These are distinct checkpoints rather than a claim of a freshly completed full-suite run.

### Environmental telemetry, time, and history

**Added and improved**

- Direct Probe T telemetry ingestion alongside existing Lamp measurements and BLE/Thread-connected data flows.
- Durable local records, historical graphs, and the option to synchronize telemetry with ARC cloud services.
- History cursor and record-identity handling, including work on deduplication, replay safety, and identity-aware transfer.
- Separate treatment of **measurement time**, **collection time**, and **phone reception time**, preserving timestamp validity and data provenance rather than inventing missing acquisition times.
- Probe-specific graph selection, sample-time handling, and local storage that retains historical measurements.
- Device time synchronization, acknowledgement handling, and sample-cadence controls as part of the firmware/app integration.
- Development of **hardware history v4** and generation-scoped history semantics across the app and backend-facing contracts. Broader backend rollout and coverage remain separate validation work.

**Verified — 12 October**

- A physical iPhone received the Probe T's time-sync acknowledgement and live environmental telemetry.
- The application stored the measurement locally and displayed the first recorded, timestamped Probe graph point.

### Lighting controls and application experience

**Improved**

- Lighting channels, power, fan controls, operational limits, schedules, and photoperiod visualization.
- Environmental dashboards, history navigation, graph presentation, and locally retained measurements.
- Device-centric **Connections** views for local hardware, registered devices, people, and projects.
- Light/dark themes, multilingual interface catalogs, and device layouts adapted to phone and tablet use.
- Cloud-connected and local-use workflows that distinguish discovery, registered ownership, and actual hardware authorization.

### ARC Secure Core — constrained-device cryptography

**Demonstrated — 13 October**

- Built a bounded secure-session protocol design for controller-scoped credentials, encrypted communication, and authenticated commands.
- Passed **35 host-side checks**, covering handshake interoperability, authenticated message handling, reconnect behavior, and rejection of invalid or replayed traffic.
- Produced an STM32WBA5-targeted preliminary footprint measurement of approximately **336 bytes of additional static RAM** and **4.1 KB of flash** for the protocol control layer. These measurements do not include the complete embedded cryptographic implementation.
- Collected real Probe T heap, task-stack, and runtime-memory measurements to guide coexistence with BLE, Thread, sensing, and retained telemetry.
- Advanced the integration strategy for **STM32WBA hardware cryptographic capabilities**, including AES, hashing, random-number generation, and hardware-assisted key handling where supported.

**Next:** complete the embedded cryptographic-provider, credential-provisioning, and authorization integration; then validate encrypted sessions between the physical device and the ARC application end to end.

Read [ARC Secure Core](docs/ARC_SECURE_CORE.md) for the development overview.

## September 2026 — Connected application foundations

### Added and advanced

- Established the working hardware-to-app path for Lamp controls, BLE communication, environmental sensing, and Thread-relayed data.
- Introduced hardware-owned operating limits and safety-aware control boundaries alongside app-side lighting schedules.
- Advanced history synchronization from retained device samples to app local storage and ARC cloud services, with record-level identity and reliability improvements.
- Built foundations for account access, registered-device inventory, and permission-aware collaboration.
- Established the iOS **TestFlight** distribution path for invited evaluation.

### ARC ecosystem

The mobile app is developed alongside the **STM32G4 lamp controller**, **STM32WBA5 wireless firmware**, **environmental probes**, **gateway technology**, and **ARC cloud services**. Milestones from those related systems are included here when they materially advance the connected app experience.
